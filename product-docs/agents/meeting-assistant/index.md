## What the Meeting Assistant agent is {#what-it-is}

Oliv AI's Meeting Assistant agent is an action agent for the people who run meetings on open deals. It works only on active opportunities. If the deal exists and the meeting is on your calendar, it runs automatically. First meetings with new contacts get a research brief instead, covered in [Prepare for a meeting](https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/oliv-ai-product-docs-v3/product-docs/workflows/prepare-for-a-meeting/). In the **Agents** marketplace it's listed as a **Pre-built** agent under **Customer Success**.

### Before the meeting {#before-the-meeting}

The brief arrives 30 minutes before the call. It's built to fit one screen and take about 60 seconds to read.

- **Deal summary.** Stage, amount, close date, last activity and next steps.
- **Qualification status.** Where the deal stands against your methodology, with gaps and open questions highlighted.
- **Stakeholders.** Who's in the meeting, the role each plays, and what they care about based on earlier conversations.
- **Recommended agenda.** Tailored to the deal stage and the qualification gaps.

The prep also reaches you on Home. When something about an upcoming meeting changes, Meeting Assistant posts a row in the **Agent Feed**. Olivia's daily brief carries a Meeting Assistant section with **Detours**, plans for objections that might come up, and a **Review meeting plan** button.

### After the meeting {#after-the-meeting}

Once the recording is processed, three things are ready for you.

- **A follow-up email draft.** Written from the transcript: what was discussed, what was agreed, and next steps with dates where possible. It isn't a template.
- **Proposed deal field updates.** Stage, amount, close date, qualification fields and next steps, based on what was said.
- **Qualification gap flags.** New information, or notable absences, that change the deal's qualification status.

## Why the Meeting Assistant agent matters {#why-it-matters}

### What it's for {#what-its-for}

An AI meeting assistant earns its place when you want to be prepared for each deal meeting and leave with the follow-up done. Oliv AI's Meeting Assistant agent handles the prep before each deal meeting and the paperwork after it. That leaves the time in between for thinking about the customer.

### How it helps {#how-it-helps}

- **You walk in ready.** The brief lands half an hour early and reads in about a minute.
- **The email is already written.** The draft comes from the real conversation, so you edit rather than start from a blank page.
- **The CRM keeps pace.** Changes heard on the call come back as CRM updates you can check on the meeting.
- **Gaps show up early.** Missing qualification details are flagged after each deal meeting, while there's time to fix them.

### What happens without it {#without-it}

Reps lose 15 to 20 minutes rebuilding context before each call. Each follow-up email takes another 15 minutes. CRM updates rely on memory, so fields drift out of date. Qualification gaps surface at the forecast review, when it's too late to act on them.

### Why buyers care {#why-buyers-care}

VPs of Sales and sales managers are the usual buyers. Account executives champion it, because they get time back before and after every deal call. The value grows with deal volume, since there's more for the gap tracking to catch.

## How deal meetings get prepared and followed up {#methods}

There are three common ways to get ready for a deal meeting and close it out afterwards. Each catches something different, and each misses something.

### Prep from the CRM record {#prep-from-crm}

You open the opportunity and read its fields. In HubSpot, for example, a [close date is the day the deal is expected to close](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/hubspots-default-deal-properties), and next step holds what you or a colleague will do next. This catches the deal's shape fast. It misses anything nobody typed in, and a field that was never updated after the last call still looks current.

### Prep from notes and past recordings {#prep-from-notes}

You reread your notes, scroll the last email thread and replay the end of the previous call. This catches what the buyer actually said and who said it. It is slow, often 15 to 20 minutes per call, and it depends on your notes being complete.

### Follow-up from memory or from the transcript {#follow-up-methods}

Writing the recap from memory is quick to start but drops small promises and dates. Writing it from the transcript catches every ask, owner and date, but by hand it takes about 15 minutes per call. Either way, someone still has to copy the changes into the CRM.

### Which of these Meeting Assistant does {#what-meeting-assistant-does}

Meeting Assistant builds its brief from the deal's CRM fields and from earlier conversations, so it combines the first two ways. After the meeting, it drafts the follow-up from the transcript and proposes the field changes, which covers the third.

## Review and send the follow-up {#review-the-follow-up}

## How to measure meeting prep and follow-up {#measure-follow-through}

Two measures tell you whether deal meetings are being prepared and closed out well. Track them per rep for a few weeks before you change anything.

- **Follow-up turnaround.** The time from the end of the call to the recap reaching the buyer. Its weak spot: a fast email can still miss what was agreed, so read a sample of them too.
- **Fields current after each meeting.** The share of deal meetings after which stage, close date and next step were checked or changed. Its weak spot: a filled field can still be wrong. HubSpot's [last activity date](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/hubspots-default-deal-properties) shows that something was logged, not whether the deal record matches the call.

### Where you see them in Meeting Assistant {#where-you-see-them}

On each meeting's page, the **Action Items** tab shows the **Next follow-up** card, which turns **Overdue** when it slips. The **CRM Updates** tab shows which changes were applied and which still wait as a **Pending Update**. Checking both after a week of meetings shows you where follow-through is stalling.

## How to prepare for and follow up a deal meeting by hand {#by-hand}

1. **Open the opportunity.** Note the [stage, which signals where the deal is in your process](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/object-settings/set-up-and-customize-pipelines), plus amount, close date and next step.
2. **Reread the last conversation.** Pull the previous call's notes or recording and list what the buyer asked for.
3. **Check qualification.** Mark which criteria of your methodology are still unconfirmed.
4. **Write an agenda.** Put the open criteria and the buyer's asks at the top.
5. **Run the meeting and take notes.** Capture asks, owners and dates as they come up.
6. **Write the recap.** Send the buyer what was discussed, what was agreed and the next steps with dates.
7. **Update the CRM.** Change stage, close date, next step and qualification fields to match the call.

What goes wrong is usually steps 6 and 7. They happen at the end of the day, from memory, after the next three calls.

### The same job with Meeting Assistant {#same-job-meeting-assistant}

Meeting Assistant does steps 1 to 4 for you and sends the result as the brief 30 minutes before the call. After the recording is processed, it drafts step 6 and proposes step 7. Your part is to read the brief, run the meeting, then review the draft and approve the updates.

### Worked example: a week of deal meetings {#worked-example}

This is an illustrative calculation with sample figures, not an Oliv AI output, and it claims no saving. Take an account executive with 12 meetings on active deals in one week. The per-meeting minutes use the status-quo ranges on Oliv AI's agent page: 15 to 20 minutes on context and 15 minutes on the follow-up.

| Task by hand | Minutes per meeting | Meetings | Minutes in the week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rebuild deal context (low end) | 15 | 12 | 15 × 12 = 180 |
| Rebuild deal context (high end) | 20 | 12 | 20 × 12 = 240 |
| Write the follow-up email | 15 | 12 | 15 × 12 = 180 |
| **Total, low end** | 30 | 12 | 180 + 180 = 360 (6 hours) |
| **Total, high end** | 35 | 12 | 240 + 180 = 420 (7 hours) |

That is 6 to 7 hours a week before any CRM updates, close to a full working day. Run the same sum with your own meeting count before deciding which steps to hand over.

## Limits and things to know {#limits}

- **It works on active opportunities.** Meetings with no linked opportunity don't get a deal brief. First meetings with new contacts are handled by research before the first call.
- **It proposes, it doesn't send.** You review, adjust and send the follow-up yourself. Field updates go through CRM Manager, which holds changes like a stage move for you to confirm.
- **It isn't a forecasting agent.** Meeting Assistant surfaces field updates. Forecaster uses them for pipeline projection.
- **It isn't a coaching tool.** Meeting Assistant is operational. Coach handles rep development.
- **It needs recordings.** The follow-up, field updates and gap flags come from Oliv Notetaker recordings and transcripts.

Most teams are connected within a week.

#### What if our CRM data is incomplete? {#faq-messy-crm}

Meeting Assistant works with CRM Manager to keep deal fields current. If data is missing or stale, CRM Manager fills it in from call history, so you don't need a clean CRM to start.

#### Is there a minimum team size? {#faq-team-size}

No. It delivers value for individual reps straight away. Qualification gap tracking gets more useful as deal volume grows.

#### Can customer success teams use it? {#faq-customer-success}

Yes, on meetings linked to an active opportunity, such as a renewal or expansion. Customer success and account management are among the teams it's built for.

#### What should a pre-call brief include? {#faq-brief-contents}

For a deal meeting, the deal summary, where qualification stands, who is attending and what they care about, and an agenda. Meeting Assistant's brief carries all four on one screen.

#### Can an AI meeting assistant update the CRM? {#faq-update-crm}

Meeting Assistant proposes the field changes it heard on the call, and CRM Manager writes them. Changes like a stage move wait for your approval on the meeting's **CRM Updates** tab.

#### Can it track MEDDPICC gaps? {#faq-meddpicc}

Yes. When MEDDPICC is the **Active** framework, the **Meeting insights** tab shows a chip for each criterion, and a card says so when a criterion wasn't discussed.
