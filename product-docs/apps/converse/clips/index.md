# Sales call clips: define the moments, then build libraries from new recordings

How to collect and share sales call clips without scrubbing recordings: define the moments, let Oliv AI clip new recordings into libraries on the Enable add-on, and share them in Slack or by link.

- Applies to: Enablement, sales managers and anyone who learns from recorded calls. Clip libraries sit in the Enable add-on.
- Status: Live
- Verified: 2026-11-03
- Canonical page: https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/oliv-ai-product-docs-v3/product-docs/apps/converse/clips/

Sales call clips teach reps what good sounds like, but finding the moments means scrubbing through recordings. Describe the moments once, and Oliv AI's Content Curator agent clips what matches in each new recording and posts each clip to Slack. If a recording has no match, it makes no clip. Clips land in a library on the **Library** tab of Enable, which is an add-on.

## Why sales call clips matter {#why-it-matters}

### What it's for {#what-its-for}

Sales call clips let a rep hear a good answer before the next call. To test whether you need them yet, name your most common objection. Then ask a rep to find a recent example of it handled well. If they can't find one without scrubbing through recordings, start now.

### How it helps {#how-it-helps}

Pick the line that sounds like your team, and set that up first:

- **Calls aren't being recorded yet.** Fix recording first, because the agent has nothing to clip until new recordings arrive.
- **New reps can't find a good objection answer.** Start with **Objections**, filed in an onboarding library.
- **Managers coach from memory.** Add a moment for the objection they raise most, filed in a coaching library.
- **Competitor answers vary from rep to rep.** Start with **Competitor Mentions**, posted to the channel your sales team reads.
- **Product keeps asking what customers want.** Start with **Product Requests**, posted to a channel the product team reads.

### What happens without it {#without-it}

If you skip sales call clips, good answers stay in recordings that nobody revisits, and each new rep works out objections alone. If no reps are ramping and nobody is asking for examples, you can wait. Run the test again before your next new hire starts.

### Why buyers care {#why-buyers-care}

Enablement leads usually own this decision, because they own the library and decide what goes in it. Sales managers should weigh in if they coach from real calls. Decide before new hires ramp, because examples are what they ask for first.

### Write a description the agent can use {#write-description}

The description is the agent's only guidance, because it has no [knowledge files](https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/oliv-ai-product-docs-v3/product-docs/agents/builder/knowledge/) to fall back on. A one-word topic leaves it guessing, so say who does what, and what makes the moment worth keeping.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| Pricing | A rep answers a pricing or security concern and keeps the deal moving. |
| Good discovery | A rep moves past surface-level questions to uncover the prospect's pain, the cost of inaction and the decision criteria. |

## How sales call moments get found and clipped {#methods}

You can get a moment out of a recording in three ways. Each one catches different things, so choose by what you want reps to learn.

### Watching and trimming by hand {#method-by-hand}

Someone watches the call, spots the moment and trims it. A person can judge a subtle moment, such as a rep recovering a call that was going badly. You miss whatever nobody has time to watch, and the folder stops growing when that person moves on.

### Searching for a word or tracked term {#method-keywords}

You search a transcript for a word and jump to where it was said. [HubSpot's tracked terms](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/calling/review-call-recordings-and-transcripts) work this way, taking you to the parts of a transcript that mention the term. This catches named competitors and pricing words. It misses the same concern in other words, and a word being said doesn't mean a rep handled it well.

### Describing the moment in plain language {#method-described}

You write down what the moment is, and software matches segments on meaning. This catches an objection however the buyer phrases it. It misses whatever your description leaves out, so your wording decides the result.

### Which of these Oliv AI does {#what-oliv-ai-does}

Oliv AI's Content Curator agent works from your description and looks for intent rather than keywords. For a moment you already know about, [Converse lets you search and then clip it](https://www.oliv.ai/converse) yourself. So you can use the agent for the moments that keep recurring, and **Clip** for the one-off.

## How to share sales call clips {#share-clips}

A clip only teaches if it reaches the rep before the next call. You have three routes, so match each to who needs the clip.

- **A channel, automatically.** Set **Channel Name** on the moment, and each new clip posts there with a direct link. Use this when a whole team should see what the moment catches.
- **A whole library.** Set **Access Type** to **Public** when a library is ready to share across teams. Keep it **Private** while you curate.
- **One clip, one person.** Press **Share** under the video. A clip's shared view opens from a link, so it also works for someone outside your workspace.

Before you share, remember what travels with the clip: the participants' names and the call date. Point each moment at a channel whose members should be hearing customer calls, and think twice before you send a link outside the company. [NN/g's guidance on research calls](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/informed-consent/) treats sharing a person's information with colleagues as a use they may not have agreed to. It's written for user research, but the check carries over.

## How to measure whether a moment is working {#measure-moments}

Two simple counts tell you whether a moment earns its place. Work them out per moment, because each moment has its own library and channel.

### Keep rate {#keep-rate}

Open a library and count how many of its recent clips you'd really send to a rep. Divide that by the number of clips you checked. A low rate usually means the description is too broad. The weak spot is judgment, so have the same person score each batch, and don't read much into a handful of clips.

### New clips over time {#clips-over-time}

Count the new clips in each library over a set period, such as a month. A count that drops to zero can mean a description is too narrow. It can also mean nothing matched, because a recording with no match makes no clip. Volume says nothing about quality, so read it next to the keep rate.

### Where to check in Oliv AI {#where-to-check}

The library's clip list gives you both counts. On the agent's **Runs** tab, you see run counts and the last run, and each run's record carries **View Clip Library** and **View Slack Alert**. So you can trace one clip to the message it sent. [Agent run history](https://maximuslabs-ai.github.io/oliv-ai-product-docs-v3/product-docs/agents/builder/run-history/) explains how to read that tab.

## How to build a sales call clip library by hand {#build-by-hand}

If you're doing this without an agent, these are the steps, and the last one is where it usually falls apart.

1. List the moments you want reps to hear, such as pricing objections and competitor mentions.
2. Ask managers which recent calls had good examples.
3. Watch each call and note the timestamp where the moment starts and ends.
4. Trim the clip in your recording tool, and check who can reach the full call. [HubSpot, for one, trims with playback handles and warns that clip viewers can also hear the entire recording](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/calling/review-call-recordings-and-transcripts).
5. Write a short note on why the clip matters, and add who was on the call and the date.
6. File the clip in a shared library that reps can find.
7. Post the link where the team will see it.
8. Repeat all of this for new calls, or the library goes stale.

### The same job with Content Curator {#same-job}

You keep step 1, and you write it down as a moment. Oliv AI's Content Curator agent then covers steps 3 to 7 for each new recording: it isolates the segment, drafts the note, attaches the details, files the clip and posts the link. Step 2 drops out, because the agent checks new recordings without being pointed at them. Your remaining job is to read the clips and sharpen any description that drifts.

### Worked example: five moments from three ready-made ones {#worked-example}

An enablement lead wants objections in two libraries, one for onboarding and one for coaching. Competitor mentions go in the same two, and product requests go in one. Each moment has one library and one channel, so each kind of moment needs one moment per library. This is an illustrative calculation with sample figures, not an Oliv AI output.

| Kind of moment | Libraries it feeds | Moments needed | Ready to use | Descriptions to write |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Objections | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 - 1 = 1 |
| Competitor mentions | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 - 1 = 1 |
| Product requests | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 - 1 = 0 |
| **Total** | 3 different libraries | 2 + 2 + 1 = 5 | 3 | 5 - 3 = 2 |

So the lead needs 5 moments, and writes 5 - 3 = 2 more descriptions. Both need to be specific, because the description is the agent's only guidance. The second objection moment, for example, should say what makes a clip right for coaching rather than repeat the word "objection".

## Limits and things to know {#limits}

- **Enable is an add-on.** Clips land on its **Library** tab, so you need it to browse and manage libraries.
- **Recordings come first.** The agent needs recordings flowing from the Oliv AI notetaker before it can clip anything.
- **New recordings only.** It acts on newly completed recordings.
- **One library and one channel per moment.** To send the same kind of moment to two places, set up two moments.
- **Two access types.** A library is either **Public** or **Private**.
- **No fixed clip length.** Clips are cut to be concise and complete, so lengths vary.
- **Moment descriptions are the only guidance.** The agent has no knowledge files, so put everything it needs into the description.
- **No match, no clip.** If a recording has nothing that matches a moment, no clip is made.

Define the moment, and Oliv AI posts each new matching clip to the Slack channel you chose, with a direct link. You can also open a library to other teams, or press **Share** on a single clip.

#### Which sales call moments are worth clipping? {#faq-worth-clipping}

Start with moments that teach or inform someone: pricing objections, competitor mentions, discovery wins, customer pain, product feedback and strong closes. Oliv AI comes with **Product Requests**, **Competitor Mentions** and **Objections** ready to use.

#### Does the agent clip automatically, or do I trigger it? {#faq-automatic}

Automatically. Once a moment is defined, Oliv AI's Content Curator agent runs when a meeting is completed and adds clips as it finds them. **Run Agent** is there if you want to start a run by hand.

#### Can I clip a moment myself? {#faq-manual}

Yes. Open the meeting's **Recording** tab in Oliv AI, find the moment and press **Clip**.

#### Will the agent clip our older recordings? {#faq-older-calls}

Plan on new recordings only. Oliv AI's Content Curator agent acts on newly completed recordings. For a moment in an older call, search for it in Converse and press **Clip** on the meeting's **Recording** tab.

#### How does it know what to clip? {#faq-what-to-clip}

You describe the moment in plain language. Oliv AI's Content Curator agent matches segments against that description, using context and intent rather than keyword lists.

#### What if it clips something irrelevant? {#faq-irrelevant}

Remove the clip and Oliv AI's Content Curator agent learns from it. You can also refine the moment's description at any time to sharpen what it looks for.

#### What does each clip include? {#faq-clip-contents}

A short note on why it was captured, the participants, the timestamp, the call date and a link to the source recording.

#### How long is a sales call clip? {#faq-length}

There's no set length. Each clip is cut to be concise and complete, so it holds the whole moment rather than a fixed number of seconds.

#### Does it work on customer success and onboarding calls? {#faq-cs-calls}

Yes. New recordings in Oliv AI are in scope whatever the call type, including sales calls, demos, onboarding sessions, QBRs and customer success check-ins.

#### Can we share a library across teams? {#faq-share-library}

Yes. Set the library's **Access Type** to **Public**. Keep it **Private** while you curate. Individual clips can also be shared with **Share**.

#### Can someone outside our workspace watch a clip? {#faq-outside-viewer}

Yes. A clip has a shared view that opens from a link, built for someone outside your workspace.

#### Where do new clips show up? {#faq-where}

In the library set for the moment, on the **Library** tab of Enable, which is an add-on. A Slack message with a direct link also goes to that moment's channel.

#### Which moments come ready to use? {#faq-default-moments}

Three come ready: **Product Requests**, **Competitor Mentions** and **Objections**. Add your own for anything else your team wants to learn from.
