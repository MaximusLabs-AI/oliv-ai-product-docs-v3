How Oliv AI prepares you for a sales meeting, from the deal brief that arrives 30 minutes before the call to first-meeting research on a new contact, and how to use both.

Preparing for a deal meeting by hand means pulling the deal status, the people and an agenda together yourself, and a first meeting adds research on a new contact. AI meeting preparation puts all of that in front of you before you join. Oliv AI sends you a brief before your sales meetings, so you walk in knowing where things stand and what to cover. For a first meeting with someone new, Oliv AI's Olivia agent researches the company and the person.

## Step 3: Read the brief in about a minute {#step-3-read-the-brief}

The deal brief is designed to fit one screen and take about 60 seconds to read. It has four parts:

| Part | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Deal summary** | Stage, amount, close date, last activity and next steps |
| **Qualification status** | Where the deal stands against your methodology, with gaps and open questions highlighted |
| **Stakeholders** | Who's in this meeting, the role each plays, and what they care about based on earlier conversations |
| **Recommended agenda** | What to cover, tailored to the deal stage and the qualification gaps |

Start with the gaps. They're the questions worth asking while there's still time to act on the answers, which is the point of [sales meeting preparation](https://www.oliv.ai/blog/meeting-preparation-for-sales).

## Why meeting preparation matters {#why-it-matters}

### What it's for {#what-its-for}

A brief tells you where the deal stands, who's in the room and what to raise. The first minutes of the call then go on the buyer, not on catching up.

### How it helps {#how-it-helps}

- **You don't have to ask.** The brief lands 30 minutes before the meeting.
- **It reads in a minute.** One screen, about 60 seconds, scannable on the way into the room.
- **Gaps show up early.** Open qualification questions appear while you can still ask them.
- **First calls get research too.** New contacts come with company and person context and an opening line.

### What happens without it {#without-it}

Without prep, reps rebuild each deal from notes and CRM records before the call, or walk in cold. Qualification gaps tend to surface at the forecast review, too late to fix. First calls open with questions the rep could have answered beforehand.

## How sales meetings get prepared {#methods}

Prep draws on four places, whether you do it by hand or a brief does it for you. Know what each misses before you rely on it in a meeting.

### The CRM record: where the deal sits {#from-crm-record}

The record gives you stage, amount, close date and last activity at a glance. It misses what the buyer actually said, and it's only as current as its last update. Defaults can mislead too: in HubSpot, a new open deal with no close date [gets the last day of the month by default](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/hubspots-default-deal-properties). That date may be nothing the buyer ever told you.

The stage needs the same care. [Gartner said in 2019 that B2B buyers often revisit buying tasks](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2019-11-13-gartner-says-the-traditional-linear-sales-process-no-) rather than moving through them in order. A stage tells you where the seller placed the deal, so check it against what the buyer last did.

### Past conversations: what people said {#from-past-conversations}

Call recordings and notes show what was agreed, who raised what and what each person cares about. They can't help with someone who hasn't spoken yet, such as a finance lead joining late. Rereading them is also the slow part of prep. Before a first meeting, there's nothing to read.

### A qualification framework: what you still don't know {#from-qualification}

Holding a deal against MEDDPICC, BANT or your team's own framework shows which questions nobody has answered. That makes it the best input for an agenda. It won't catch anything the framework doesn't ask, and a filled-in item can still rest on a weak answer.

### Outside research: who you're meeting {#from-outside-research}

The company's site, funding news and the contact's LinkedIn are all you have when no deal exists yet. They give you a specific way to open the call. They can't tell you what this person thinks of your product, and public profiles can be out of date.

## How to prepare for a sales call by hand {#prepare-by-hand}

To prepare for a sales call, find out where the deal stands, who's joining and what you still need to learn. Then turn that into an agenda. Before a first meeting, swap the deal check for research on the company and the person. For more on the planning itself, read this [sales call planning guide](https://www.oliv.ai/blog/sales-call-planning-guide).

Here's the routine, in order:

1. **Sort your calendar.** Mark each meeting as a live deal, a first meeting or internal, because each one needs different prep.
2. **Open the CRM record for each deal meeting.** Note the stage, amount, close date, last activity and next steps. In HubSpot, [the last activity date shows the latest note, call, email or meeting logged on the deal](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/hubspots-default-deal-properties), so an old date warns you the rest may be stale.
3. **Reread the last conversation.** Find what was agreed and who owes what.
4. **List who's joining.** Write down each person's role and what they've cared about so far, and look hardest at anyone new.
5. **Check qualification against your framework.** Write each open item as a question you can ask.
6. **Write the agenda from those questions.** Roger Schwarz argues in Harvard Business Review that [a good agenda sets out what has to happen before the meeting as well as during it](https://hbr.org/2015/03/how-to-design-an-agenda-for-an-effective-meeting). If the buyer needs to bring something, send the agenda early enough for them to do it.
7. **For a first meeting, research instead.** Check what the company does, its size, funding and recent news, then the person's career and role. Pick one specific point to open with.

What usually goes wrong is time. On a full day, steps 2 to 5 are often the first to go, and the rep walks in having read only the calendar invite.

### Worked example: sorting a week of 12 meetings {#worked-example}

This is an illustrative calculation with sample figures, not an Oliv AI output, and it says nothing about time saved. A rep sorts next week's 12 meetings. Seven are linked to active opportunities, three are first meetings and two are internal.

| Meeting type | Meetings | What the rep gets | Share as first sorted | Share after adding the missed deal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Linked to an active opportunity | 7 | A deal brief | 7 / 12 = 58% | 8 / 12 = 67% |
| First meeting | 3 | Research from Olivia | 3 / 12 = 25% | 2 / 12 = 17% |
| Internal | 2 | Neither | 2 / 12 = 17% | 2 / 12 = 17% |

Shares are rounded, which is why the last column adds up to 101%. One of the three first meetings turns out to belong to a live deal nobody had entered in the CRM. Left alone, the rep would walk in with company research and no deal status.

Adding the deal lifts deal briefs from 7 to 8, or 67% of the week. So before each week starts, read down your first meetings and ask whether any of them belongs to a deal you already know about.

## How to check your meeting prep is working {#measure-prep}

Two simple counts tell you whether prep is doing its job. Neither needs more than your calendar and the briefs themselves.

### Brief coverage {#brief-coverage}

Take your customer meetings for the week and count how many arrived with the right kind of prep. In the worked example, that's all 10 customer meetings, with 8 deal briefs and 2 research briefs. The weak spot is that coverage counts what arrived, not what you read. It can also look fine while hiding a deal missing from the CRM, because that meeting still counts as a first meeting.

### Gaps still open at the next meeting {#gaps-still-open}

Pick one deal and compare the highlighted gaps in this brief with those in the next one. If the same gap shows up twice, the question either wasn't asked or wasn't answered. Don't expect every gap to close in one call, because a question for the economic buyer has to wait until that person joins.

### Where to see these {#where-to-see-prep}

Qualification status sits in each deal brief, so you can compare gaps from one meeting to the next. Managers see each rep's meetings, with the deal behind them, in the **Morning Brief** in **Reports › My Reports**. Count coverage from your calendar.

## What to check when a brief is missing or looks wrong {#brief-check}

Work through these in order when a meeting starts in 30 minutes and you have nothing, or the brief doesn't match the deal you know:

1. **Is the meeting linked to an active opportunity?** Deal briefs cover only those meetings, so a live deal missing from your CRM won't get one. Add the deal.
2. **Is it really a first meeting?** A new contact gets research on the company and the person, not deal status.
3. **Are your connections in place?** The brief needs your calendar, your recorder and your CRM connected.
4. **Do you need prep right now?** Don't wait for a scheduled brief. Open **Home**, keep the **Ask** tab on Oliv AI's Olivia agent, and type the call you're preparing for.
5. **Does a stage or date look stale?** Check it against the CRM record and your last call. Oliv AI's CRM Manager agent fills in missing or stale fields from call history as it goes. If the record still looks off, confirm the detail on the call.

## Limits and things to know {#limits}

- **Deal briefs need a deal.** The deal brief covers meetings linked to an active opportunity in your CRM. Meetings with new contacts get the research brief instead.
- **Connections come first.** No brief arrives until your calendar, recorder and CRM are connected.
- **The research brief is short by design.** It's three bullets and an icebreaker. The full detail view is in the platform.
- **Emails wait for your choice.** A pre-meeting or follow-up email goes out only when you pick a send option or press send.

## Common questions {#common-questions}

#### When does the brief arrive? {#faq-when}

Thirty minutes before the meeting. You don't need to request it; it's sent automatically for meetings linked to an active opportunity.

#### How long is the brief? {#faq-length}

One screen, about 60 seconds to read. It covers the deal summary, qualification status, stakeholder context and a recommended agenda.

#### What do I get before a first meeting with someone new? {#faq-first-meeting}

Three bullets on the company and the person, plus an icebreaker. Oliv AI's Olivia agent sends it 30 minutes before the meeting.

#### Which sales methodologies does the brief use? {#faq-methodologies}

MEDDPICC, MEDDIC, SPICED, BANT or DRIVE. Your team marks one as **Active** in **Settings › Qualification Framework**, and the brief maps the deal against it.

#### Can AI build an agenda for a sales meeting? {#faq-agenda}

Yes. The recommended agenda in the deal brief is tailored to the deal stage and its qualification gaps, so it points you at what still needs answering. Check it against what you planned before you join.

#### Do I need a clean CRM before the brief works? {#faq-clean-crm}

No. Oliv AI's CRM Manager agent fills in missing or stale fields from call history as it goes, so the brief can start straight away.

#### Where do briefs and alerts show up? {#faq-where}

Oliv AI's Olivia agent works through Slack, Teams or the Oliv AI product. In the product, you ask her on the Home screen's Ask tab, and managers read Deal Driver's briefs in My Reports.

#### Is the follow-up email a template? {#faq-follow-up-template}

No. Oliv AI's Meeting Assistant agent writes the draft from the call transcript: what was discussed, what was agreed and what happens next. You review, adjust and send.

#### Can managers get prep for their 1:1s? {#faq-one-on-ones}

Yes. Oliv AI's Deal Driver agent prepares a One-on-One Prep report for each rep on that rep's set day, such as Thursday or Friday. Its Morning Brief covers the team's meetings every weekday.

#### Is there a minimum team size? {#faq-team-size}

No. A single rep gets value from the first brief. Gap tracking becomes more useful as deal volume grows, because there's more for it to catch.

#### How do you prepare for a sales call? {#faq-prepare-sales-call}

Find out where the deal stands, who's joining and what you still need to learn, then turn that into an agenda. Before a first meeting, research the company and the person instead, and pick one specific point to open with.

#### What should you research before a first sales meeting? {#faq-research-first-meeting}

Research the company and the person. For the company, check what it does, its size, funding and recent news. For the person, check career history, role, tenure and common ground. Oliv AI's Olivia agent sends the short version as three bullets and an icebreaker.

#### Why didn't my meeting get a deal brief? {#faq-no-brief}

Deal briefs cover meetings linked to an active opportunity, so first check that the deal is in your CRM. Then check that your calendar, recorder and CRM are connected. You can still ask Oliv AI's Olivia agent for prep on the Home screen's Ask tab.
